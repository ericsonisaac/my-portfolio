Isaac Ericson

Medical student at UCLA. Former SpaceX medical intern. Working on how medicine gets delivered when there's no hospital nearby — in orbit, on the Moon, or in the backcountry.

I'm an MD candidate at the David Geffen School of Medicine (Class of 2028), wrapping up my core clinical clerkships. Before medical school I spent nearly two years at SpaceX, where I designed the Dragon commercial medical kit and trained the crews of Inspiration4, Polaris Dawn, and the Axiom ISS missions to use it.

My research is operational space medicine: pharmacologic countermeasures, drug production during long missions, intracranial pressure in microgravity, and surgical readiness for lunar EVA. Clinically I'm drawn to emergency medicine and trauma. I also founded UCLA's wilderness medicine group and co-direct its aerospace medicine group.

Los Angeles, CA

npj MicrogravityJuly 2026

On-Demand Peptide Therapeutics for Multi-Year Space Exploration: Analysis of Clinical and Operational Relevance and Recombinant Production Feasibility

Donovan JJ, Ericson I, Wenthe A, Kovacs K, Emsley J, Williams PM. doi:10.1038/s41526-026-00630-z

Evaluated 26 candidate peptide therapeutics for clinical relevance on multi-year missions and the feasibility of producing them on demand aboard a spacecraft using engineered Bacillus subtilis. Teriparatide came out as the lead candidate.
